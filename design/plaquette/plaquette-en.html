Democracy needs a network

Democracy Together is a multinational initiative: a worldwide network of think tanks, open to young people, attentive to Africa and Europe.

Democracy is in crisis

Understood as respecting freedoms, the state and the rule of law, democracy is in crisis in France, in many other countries, and within the international community.

Causes and symptoms abound

  • Rise of populism
  • Erosion of democratic institutions
  • Foreign interference
  • Socio-economic inequality
  • Impacts of climate change
Liberal democracy must be defended today, but we must also prepare its evolution, or even its refounding.

Several think tanks dedicated to democracy are already working toward this. But many operate in isolation, focused on the problems of the Western world: their impact is reduced, and the issues facing emerging countries are minimized.

02

Our response: a worldwide network of think tanks

We are convinced there is room for a multinational initiative built on a network of think tanks. We have named it Democracy Together.

Open to young people

The network makes room for them and seeks to involve them.

Worldwide

It brings together developed and emerging countries.

Attentive to Africa and Europe

Two continents with very different challenges, but linked destinies.

Our wager: a collective democratic intelligence, to meet present and future global challenges, as part of restoring an international order that respects and is respected.

03

To become a global reference for advancing liberal democratic thought.

By creating an inclusive ecosystem, where innovative ideas contribute to more resilient and more equitable societies.

Five missions

  1. Spark new thinking.
  2. Gather this thinking, share it, and open it to debate.
  3. Build capacity, especially in developing countries, through training, exchanges, and targeted funding.
  4. Support youth involvement.
  5. Bring together scientific, diplomatic and economic perspectives, and promote geographic, cultural and gender diversity in democratic debate.

A vast field

Institutional questions and questions of democratic life, which arise differently from country to country:

  • education
  • health
  • environment
  • standards
  • the role of AI
  • digital governance
  • the place of science
  • citizen participation
  • fighting corruption
  • democratic transitions

The contributions gathered are also intended to become objects of research, for academia and beyond.

04

Short contributions, quality-controlled

This is how we will build this collective intelligence: through the exchange and sharing of short contributions.

Short

500 to 1,000 words

Many contributions are expected, and a relevant, well-considered idea can always be expressed concisely.

Quality

Quality is controlled, to favor the most significant, useful and innovative ideas.

Peer
reviewed

Who judges quality? Not us!

Peers, chosen by the contributors themselves. It’s one of the distinctive features of our initiative.

05

KOHOP,
the network’s platform

Bilingual, soon multilingual, KOHOP will publish members' contributions. Its principle: peer judgment, in the manner of a scientific journal.

  1. The author submits a contribution

    500 to 1,000 words, with links to more detailed texts, available in one click.

  2. The author names reviewers

    No anonymity: their names and their analyses are published alongside the text.

  3. The editor-in-chief oversees

    They can disqualify a reviewer who is too close to the author, a co-author for example.

  4. The author revises

    2 weeks

    They receive the feedback and decide whether to take it into account.

  5. The editor-in-chief decides

    They accept or decline the publication. Two positive reviews amount to near-automatic acceptance, barring obvious excess or a breach of the charter.

A wager on intellectual honesty and transparency.

  • Published texts belong to the contributors, not to the platform.
  • Free and open access for readers.
  • Publishing on KOHOP requires joining the network.
  • The platform quickly widens the network's circle of “insiders”.

Your think tank has a place in the network

yours

Any institution with think tank activity can join, even if it also carries out other work, such as training. Membership requires signing a short charter.

What the network offers you

  • Greater visibility for your work.
  • Streamlined administration, as much as possible, for emerging think tanks.
  • Training, exchanges and targeted funding.
  • Conferences, symposiums and research projects.
  • Complete freedom of action, and publications that remain yours.
  • A fund dedicated to collaborative projects, coming soon.
